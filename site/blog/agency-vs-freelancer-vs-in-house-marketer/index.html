Agency vs Freelancer vs In-House Marketer: Which Should You Hire?

By Emmanuella Omeh·Published 15 November 2026·6 min read
Hiring
Quick answer

Hire an agency when you need coordinated marketing services, a freelancer for a defined specialist responsibility, or an in-house marketer for an embedded role. Compare the actual scope, skills, management requirements and total cost of each option. Your best choice depends on the gap you need filled and the support you can provide.

You need help with marketing, but the job description keeps growing. First it was social media. Now you need better ads, a landing page and someone to follow up with enquiries.

Should you hire one person, find a freelancer or work with an agency?

Start by defining the responsibility. A job title can hide an unrealistic combination of tasks. A service package can do the same. This guide helps you compare the options against what your business actually needs.

What is the main difference between these options?

The useful difference is how the work is organised and managed. Compare who will make decisions, carry out specialist tasks and coordinate delivery in each proposed arrangement.

Use this as a decision framework:

Option Consider it for Clarify before choosing
Agency Several connected marketing responsibilities Named team, scope and coordination
Freelancer A defined skill or deliverable stream Availability, dependencies and handover
In-house marketer An embedded ongoing role Skill coverage, tools and support
Combined arrangement Gaps around an existing team Decision rights and overlapping work

Avoid assuming an agency includes every discipline, a freelancer works alone or an employee can cover all channels. Ask each candidate to describe the actual delivery setup.

When should you consider a marketing agency?

Consider an agency when your brief involves several connected services and you want a clear owner for coordinating them. Check that the proposed team really covers those services.

For a coach, you might need content, ad creative, a sales page and nurturing emails to support the same programme. For an e-commerce business, you might need product messaging, campaigns and follow-up organised around the same offer.

Ask the agency:

  • Who develops the strategy?
  • Who creates the content and manages campaigns?
  • Who checks the website or landing page?
  • Who combines the findings into recommendations?
  • What work remains with our team?

At Artlify, our scope can cover Meta ads, social media, content and funnels. The appropriate combination should be agreed around your business problem and capacity.

When should you consider a freelancer?

Consider a freelancer when you can define a specialist responsibility and provide the context or coordination it needs. Assess the individual's experience and working process against that assignment.

For example, you might already have a strategy and need someone to edit video, manage a specific campaign or write emails. In that situation, clarify who provides the brief and approves the work.

Ask about capacity, turnaround, revisions, access and availability. If the work depends on other people, map those dependencies before starting.

The key question is whether you have someone who can manage the wider picture. A specialist can deliver excellent work within a clear brief while decisions about the offer, budget or customer journey remain your responsibility.

When should you consider an in-house marketer?

Consider an in-house marketer when you want an embedded role with ongoing access to business decisions and internal teams. Design the role around a realistic set of responsibilities and provide the support it requires.

Before recruiting, write down what the person must do personally and what they can coordinate through others.

Discuss:

  1. The primary business objective.
  2. The channels they will own.
  3. Their creative and technical responsibilities.
  4. The tools and budget available.
  5. Who approves strategy and spending.
  6. When outside specialists may be needed.

Avoid turning a list of every marketing task into a single role without checking skill requirements. Ask candidates to explain their strengths, limitations and approach to working with additional support.

How should you compare the total cost?

Compare the complete cost of delivering the agreed work, including any support each option still needs. Use actual quotes and employment costs rather than invented market averages.

Create a comparison sheet with these rows:

  • Provider fee or employment cost.
  • Recruitment or onboarding expenses where applicable.
  • Content production.
  • Tools and subscriptions.
  • Media spend.
  • Specialist work outside the main scope.
  • Internal time needed for coordination and approvals.

Employment-related obligations depend on the jurisdiction and arrangement.

Artlify's fees typically range from $500 to $10,000 (USD), depending on scope. Depending on the engagement, that's quoted as a monthly fee or as a total for the agreed work.

Request a scoped quote. A headline figure cannot tell you whether the proposed support is affordable or sufficient.

Who will manage the work day to day?

Agree on one owner for priorities, feedback and approvals regardless of the hiring model. Ask who will keep delivery moving when several contributors are involved.

You can use a simple responsibility list:

  • Business owner: Confirms the offer, commercial constraints and budget.
  • Marketing lead: Coordinates the agreed plan.
  • Specialists: Carry out assigned work.
  • Approver: Returns consolidated feedback.

Check your own availability before hiring. If you cannot review content, nominate someone who can. If your product information changes frequently, decide how updates will reach the people creating your marketing.

Clear responsibilities make the arrangement easier to evaluate and manage.

What evidence should you request from each candidate?

Request examples that show relevant judgement, a clear contribution and an honest account of the outcome. Apply the same standard to an agency, freelancer or employee candidate.

Ask them to explain the problem, their role, what they changed and what happened. If they show team work, ask which parts they personally handled.

Artlify's work with Coach of Success involved improving the content direction and building a funnel from ad creative through the landing page and nurturing emails. Her original challenge was attracting qualified leads alongside a social presence that needed optimisation.

The result: she landed her first five clients for her $5,000 coaching programme.

Ask every candidate for the same three things: the problem, their contribution and a verified result.

Can you combine an agency with your own team?

Yes, a combined arrangement is an option if you define the boundaries and agree how decisions move between contributors. Start by listing the strengths your business already has.

You might retain content production internally and seek help with campaign strategy and execution. Or an internal marketing lead might coordinate outside specialists around launches.

Before starting, agree on:

  1. The owner of each channel and asset.
  2. Who creates the brief.
  3. Who can approve changes.
  4. Where files and performance information are kept.
  5. Who reports on the overall objective.

Ask what happens when recommendations conflict. Someone must have the authority to settle priorities without leaving contributors waiting or duplicating each other's work.

What should happen when the arrangement ends?

Agree on handover, access and ownership before work begins. Make sure you can identify what will be delivered and what must be transferred when the relationship ends.

For an agency or freelancer, ask about editable files, documentation and third-party materials. For an internal role, decide how ongoing work and credentials are documented.

Artlify provides a complete folder of marketing materials and the marketing strategy document during handover. Its minimum contract is three months with one month's cancellation notice, subject to that minimum term.

Clients keep their ad accounts, pages and all the content created for their brand.

Treat handover as part of the service evaluation rather than a question left until cancellation.

How do you make the final hiring decision?

Choose the arrangement that covers your priority responsibilities at a manageable cost and with a clear working process. Compare candidates against the same brief.

Use a short decision process:

  1. Write your main business problem in one sentence.
  2. List the responsibilities required to address it.
  3. Mark what your team can already handle.
  4. Request proposals or assess candidates for the remaining gap.
  5. Check evidence, costs, communication and handover.

If a proposal introduces services you did not request, ask why they are needed. If it excludes something essential, ask who will cover it. Choose only when you understand how the full assignment will be managed.

Frequently asked questions

Is an agency better than a freelancer?

An agency is a suitable option when its proposed team and scope match the responsibilities you need covered. A freelancer may suit a defined specialist assignment. Compare relevant experience, coordination, cost and availability, and check which tasks remain with your own team before deciding.

Can one in-house marketer do everything?

Define the role and assess the candidate's actual skill coverage before assigning every marketing task to one person. Ask what they can deliver directly and where they would need support. Provide a clear objective, realistic priorities and an agreed budget for the work.
