How Much Should You Spend on Facebook and Instagram Ads?

By Emmanuella Omeh·Published 15 November 2026·6 min read
Meta Ads
Quick answer

Set your Facebook and Instagram ads budget using your margins, acquisition costs, sales capacity and available cash. Start with a defined test you can fund, then review the business outcome before increasing spend. Keep media spend, agency fees and production costs visible so a campaign's sales figures do not hide an unprofitable overall commitment.

Before asking how much to spend on ads, ask how much your business can afford to pay for a customer. That gives your budget a commercial starting point.

An online store and a coaching business need different inputs. The store must account for products, fulfilment and returns. The coach must consider programme delivery, sales handling and capacity.

This guide gives you a method and a benchmark to start from. Use your own figures, identify the assumptions and decide what evidence would justify further investment.

Is there a right Facebook ads budget for every business?

No single amount suits every business. Choose a budget that fits a defined objective, your financial constraints and the test you want to run.

Begin with these questions:

  • What action do we want someone to take?
  • What is a completed sale worth after direct costs?
  • What do we currently know about acquisition costs?
  • Can we handle the enquiries or orders?
  • What can we fund without affecting essential operations?

If you have no campaign history, label cost assumptions as assumptions. Ask whoever manages the campaign to explain how the test is expected to produce useful information within the amount available.

Avoid choosing a number because a competitor or creator mentioned it. You need a plan that relates to your own offer.

One benchmark worth knowing: Meta says an ad set generally needs about 50 optimisation events, such as purchases or leads, within seven days to exit the learning phase. So a practical starting daily budget is roughly your expected cost per result × 50 ÷ 7, if your cash flow allows it. If that number is out of reach, optimise for a more frequent action or narrow the test to one offer.

What should an e-commerce brand calculate first?

Calculate the contribution from an order before advertising, then decide what portion can support customer acquisition. Use actual direct costs rather than treating selling price as available profit.

A starting calculation is:

Order revenue − product cost − other variable order costs = contribution before advertising.

Include the relevant items for your business:

  • Product or inventory cost.
  • Packaging.
  • Transaction charges.
  • Delivery subsidy paid by the business.
  • Discounts.
  • Expected refunds, returns or failed-delivery costs using your records.

This is a planning contribution calculation, not a complete accounting statement. Decide how overhead and the profit you want to retain will be covered before choosing your acquisition target.

If you sell different products, calculate by product or order mix where practical. Do not assume one margin applies to every order.

How should a coaching business plan an ads budget?

Start with programme economics, delivery capacity and the route from enquiry to enrolment. Set a budget against completed sales and qualified opportunities, not lead volume alone.

Write down:

  1. The amount collected per enrolment.
  2. Direct delivery and sales costs.
  3. The number of clients you can serve.
  4. What makes an enquiry qualified.
  5. How calls and sales are recorded.
  6. The cash available during the test.

Use your verified enquiry-to-sale rate where available. If you do not have one, avoid borrowing a conversion rate from another business and presenting it as a forecast.

Define the assumptions you will test. Ask how the budget decision changes if fewer suitable prospects book or attend than expected.

How do you work backwards from a sales goal?

Multiply the target number of acquired customers by your planned media cost per acquisition, then check whether the resulting spend is affordable. Treat the result as a scenario, not a promise.

Planned media budget = target acquired customers × assumed media cost per acquisition.

Use a cost from your own records if it is relevant to the current offer and conditions. Otherwise mark the acquisition cost as unverified.

Build a planning sheet with fields for:

  • Desired sales.
  • Acquisition-cost assumption.
  • Proposed media budget.
  • Agency and production costs.
  • Contribution remaining under the scenario.
  • Cash required before receipts arrive.

Run an alternative scenario with a less favourable acquisition cost. If that version is unaffordable, revise the spend, scope or sales target before launching.

What is the difference between media cost and total acquisition cost?

Media cost measures the advertising spend assigned to acquisition, while a broader acquisition calculation includes other chosen acquisition expenses. State your definition whenever reporting a customer cost.

Use the comparison below:

Measure Calculation for this plan Use it to assess
Media cost per acquired customer Media spend ÷ acquired customers Campaign media efficiency
Cost per qualified enquiry Relevant spend ÷ qualified enquiries The agreed qualification stage
Broader acquisition cost Included acquisition expenses ÷ new customers The wider acquisition commitment
Contribution after media Contribution before media − media spend Amount left before other excluded costs

Choose the time period and included expenses consistently. If a report uses all orders as the denominator, do not label the result a new-customer acquisition cost without confirming which buyers are new.

What should your initial advertising test include?

Define one commercial question, the offer being tested and the evidence you will review. Choose a scope that your available budget and team can support.

A practical test brief should contain:

  1. The audience or customer problem.
  2. The offer and destination.
  3. The creative ideas being compared.
  4. The primary action being measured.
  5. The spend approval and review dates.
  6. The condition for pausing or changing the test.

Avoid introducing more changes than you can interpret. If the message, offer and destination all change together, record that clearly instead of attributing the outcome to one detail.

At Artlify, we use around 30 days as an initial paid-advertising review period, depending on the service.

What should you check before increasing ad spend?

Check the business outcome, measurement quality and capacity to deliver before approving more spend. Ask what evidence supports the increase and what will be watched afterwards.

Review:

  • Completed purchases or qualified opportunities.
  • Contribution and acquisition costs.
  • Tracking compared with business records.
  • Available inventory or programme capacity.
  • Customer response and fulfilment.
  • Cash available for the next period.

A sales figure alone does not answer whether the business can sustain the plan. Ask what remains after the costs included in your calculation and identify anything excluded.

Agree on a review point after a budget change. If the outcome weakens, investigate the cause rather than assuming that a further increase is the next step.

What if the budget is too small for the proposed plan?

Reduce the scope or postpone the campaign until you can fund a meaningful test. Ask which question can be answered responsibly with the resources available.

Options to discuss include:

  • Promoting one offer instead of several.
  • Improving the sales page before buying traffic.
  • Preparing stronger product or founder content.
  • Fixing enquiry handling.
  • Gathering better cost and sales records.

Ask the campaign manager to explain the proposed order and dependencies.

You may decide to invest in preparation first. That is a valid commercial decision if the alternative is funding media while essential parts of the buying journey remain unresolved.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I spend on ads each day?

Start from your expected cost per result. Meta says an ad set generally needs about 50 optimisation events in seven days to exit the learning phase, so (cost per result × 50) ÷ 7 is a useful starting daily budget per ad set. If that's more than you can fund, test one offer at a time or optimise for a more frequent action.

Can I guarantee sales by increasing my budget?

Do not treat a higher budget as a sales guarantee. Approve increases using evidence from the campaign and the business, including acquisition costs, contribution and fulfilment capacity. Agree on a review point and what would trigger a change in the plan.

Should I include agency fees when checking profitability?

Include agency fees when assessing the total marketing commitment and the relevant period's profitability. Keep media-only calculations clearly labelled so they are not mistaken for a complete business result. Decide which expenses belong in each measure and use the same definitions when comparing periods.
