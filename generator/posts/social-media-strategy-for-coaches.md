Your content can sound thoughtful while leaving a prospective client unclear about what you sell. They may understand your topic but have no idea whether your programme fits their situation.

Start your strategy with that gap. Define what someone should understand about your work and what they can do when they are interested.

This guide adapts the [small-business social media playbook](/blog/social-media-marketing-strategy-small-business/) to a coaching offer. It focuses on suitability, offer clarity and the path from content to a conversation or purchase.

## What should social media achieve for a coach?

Choose a purpose tied to your coaching business and define how you will assess it. A useful plan can include teaching, explaining suitability and inviting qualified applications.

Write your goal in plain language. For example: “Help prospective clients understand the programme and decide whether to apply.” Then define the evidence you will review.

Distinguish between:

- The content you publish.
- The audience response.
- The enquiries you receive.
- The people who are suitable.
- The clients who enrol.

You may not be able to connect every sale confidently to a particular post. Record the information you do have and avoid claiming attribution you cannot demonstrate. Ask new enquiries how they found you where that fits your process.

## How do you define a qualified coaching lead?

Define a qualified lead using the suitability conditions of your real offer. Agree on the criteria before deciding whether a campaign or content plan is bringing the right enquiries.

Consider:

1. The problem your programme addresses.
2. The stage the person is at.
3. The support they expect.
4. Any necessary prerequisites.
5. Whether the price and delivery model fit.
6. Their readiness to take the next step.

These are prompts, not universal exclusion rules. Choose only criteria relevant to the programme and apply them consistently.

Then use the definition in your content and application process. If people repeatedly misunderstand the offer, review the way it is explained rather than simply labelling them poor leads.

## What should a coach's profile explain?

Your profile should make it clear who you help, what you offer and where an interested person should go next. Review it as someone who has never encountered your business before.

Check:

- Your name and role are understandable.
- The audience and offer are clear.
- The linked destination is current.
- Programme details are easy to find.
- Evidence is accurate and permission has been obtained.
- Contact or application instructions are clear.

Use only current information. If applications are closed, do not direct people to an unexplained dead end. If you have several programmes, decide how the linked destination helps someone choose.

Avoid promising a client outcome in the profile unless you can substantiate the wording and it accurately represents your offer.

## What content pillars work for a coaching strategy?

Choose pillars that help prospective clients understand the problem, your approach and the offer. Give each pillar a distinct role instead of collecting broad motivational topics.

Use this framework:

| Pillar | Question it answers | Example to adapt |
|---|---|---|
| Problem clarity | What is happening in my situation? | Explain a common issue you encounter |
| Practical teaching | What can I understand or try? | Walk through a relevant decision |
| Approach | How do you work? | Explain the structure of your support |
| Evidence | What can you verify? | Share an approved client experience |
| Offer clarity | Is this programme suitable? | Explain prerequisites and delivery |
| Next step | How do I proceed? | Explain the application process |

Use your own expertise to select examples. Do not manufacture client stories to fill a pillar. If the evidence is not available, choose a teaching or process post instead.

## How much should you teach for free?

Decide what your free content helps someone understand and what the paid programme actually provides. Explain that distinction honestly without making every educational post an incomplete teaser.

For example, your content might explain a decision framework while the paid offer includes personalised feedback, implementation support or a defined curriculum. Publish that distinction only if it reflects the real programme.

Review your content ideas with these questions:

- Is the lesson useful on its own?
- Does it relate to the audience I serve?
- Am I overstating what one post can achieve?
- Is the paid offer described accurately?

Avoid suggesting that people must buy because they are incapable of acting independently. Give them enough clarity to make an informed decision about the support they need.

## How should you share testimonials and client stories?

Share only experiences you can verify and have permission to publish. Explain the relevant context and your contribution without turning one person's experience into a promise for everyone.

A case-study brief should contain:

1. The original challenge.
2. The agreed work.
3. Your actual contribution.
4. The verified outcome.
5. The timeframe and relevant limitations.

## How do you connect content to your application process?

Give each relevant call to action a clear destination and explain what happens next. Match the invitation to the level of commitment you are asking for.

For example, a post explaining programme suitability might direct viewers to a page with requirements and an application. The page should answer the questions needed to make that decision.

Check the journey:

- Is the programme information current?
- Are price or payment expectations explained appropriately?
- Does the form ask relevant questions?
- Is there a confirmation message?
- Who reviews and responds to the application?
- How will unanswered enquiries be handled?

Make this a documented process before promoting applications. For the build itself, read the [sales funnel guide](/blog/sales-funnel-guide/).

## Which platforms should a coach focus on?

You don't need to be everywhere. Choose one main platform where your ideal clients already spend time, and one supporting platform. Many coaches do well with Instagram for community and LinkedIn for professional audiences. Get good at one before adding more.

## How should you build a coaching content calendar?

Organise the calendar around your offer, recurring questions and realistic production capacity. Leave room to respond to questions that emerge from actual conversations.

Use a repeatable process:

1. Select the offer or audience priority.
2. Choose specific questions within your pillars.
3. Decide which ideas need a founder video or demonstration.
4. Gather approved evidence and programme details.
5. Draft content and calls to action.
6. Review the linked journey before publication.

Choose a publishing schedule your team can maintain. Consistency beats intensity: three posts and a few stories each week will do more than bursts of daily posting followed by silence. If recording is a constraint, plan a session around a prepared question list rather than relying on spontaneous filming.

Keep offer deadlines and availability verified. Do not invent scarcity to make the calendar more persuasive.

## How do you measure a coaching social media strategy?

Review the audience response alongside the quality and progress of enquiries. Decide in advance how your team will record each stage.

Track what you can verify:

- Relevant questions and conversations.
- Visits to the intended programme destination.
- Applications and suitable applicants.
- Booked and attended calls where applicable.
- Enrolments and reasons for non-enrolment.

Label anything based on self-report or incomplete attribution. If a post attracts questions from unsuitable prospects, inspect the message and offer explanation before deciding what to change.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should a coach post on social media?

Post useful teaching, offer explanations, relevant process information and verified evidence that address your audience's real questions. Give each post a clear purpose and an appropriate next step. Choose examples from your own expertise and avoid inventing client experiences or outcomes to fill the calendar.

### Should every coaching post sell the programme?

Give each post a purpose within the wider strategy rather than forcing the same sales message into every idea. Some posts can teach or clarify suitability; others can explain the offer and application process. Review the full calendar to ensure interested people can understand how to proceed.

### How do I get better-qualified enquiries?

Start by defining suitability and reviewing whether your content, offer page and application process communicate it clearly. Record where enquiries fall outside the scope and use that information to investigate changes. This is a planning process rather than a guarantee that every future enquiry will qualify.
