You're ready to get help with marketing. But after reading several agency websites, everyone seems to offer the same thing: strategy, content, ads and growth.

How do you decide who can actually help your business?

Start with what you need the agency to take responsibility for. A coach who needs qualified sales calls has a different brief from a fashion brand trying to make online orders profitable.

This guide explains how to choose a marketing agency, compare your options and recognise a proposal that deserves a closer look.

## What should you know before hiring a marketing agency?

Before hiring an agency, define the business problem, your main goal and the resources available to support the work. You don't need a finished marketing strategy, but you should be able to explain what needs to change.

"Help us grow" leaves too much open to interpretation. Give the agency a more useful starting point:

- For a coach: "We need enquiries from people who understand our offer and can afford the programme."
- For an e-commerce brand: "We need to understand whether our advertising leaves enough profit after fulfilment."
- For either business: "We need consistent marketing without the founder managing every task."

Then prepare a brief covering your offer, audience, current activity, budget and main obstacle.

Be honest about your capacity. If you cannot film content regularly or respond to enquiries promptly, include that information. Ask the agency to explain how its proposed approach accounts for those constraints.

## Should you hire an agency, a freelancer or an in-house marketer?

Choose the arrangement that matches the responsibilities you need covered and the management capacity you have. Compare the actual people, scope and costs rather than assuming one option is automatically better.

Use this table to guide your questions:

| Option | Consider it when | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marketing agency | You want several marketing activities managed together | Who handles each service and who coordinates the work |
| Freelancer | You have a defined task or need a particular skill | Scope, availability and dependencies on other specialists |
| In-house marketer | You want someone embedded in daily business decisions | Experience, tools and additional support they will need |
| Combined approach | You already have some skills covered internally | Ownership, handovers and overlapping responsibilities |

For example, if you already have a capable content creator, you might seek help specifically with strategy, paid campaigns and conversion.

Ask every candidate to explain where their responsibility begins and ends.

## What services should your marketing agency provide?

Your agency should recommend services that address your stated problem and explain how those services connect. Ask why each activity belongs in the proposal.

For a coaching business, discuss whether the scope needs to cover:

- Offer positioning and messaging.
- Content that explains the programme.
- A landing page or application form.
- Email follow-up.
- Paid campaigns and reporting.

For an e-commerce business, discuss product content, advertising, the purchase journey and customer follow-up.

## How can you check whether an agency has relevant experience?

Ask the agency to walk you through work that resembles your business challenge. Look for an explanation of its decisions, responsibilities and results.

A useful case study discussion should cover:

1. **The starting point:** What was happening before the work began?
2. **The objective:** What was the agency asked to improve?
3. **The scope:** Which parts did its team handle?
4. **The approach:** What did it change, and why?
5. **The outcome:** What happened, over what period and with what limitations?

If someone shares a revenue screenshot, ask what costs sit behind it. If they share lead numbers, ask how lead quality was assessed.

## How much should you budget for a marketing agency?

Build your budget around the agreed scope and the total cost of carrying it out. Ask for a written breakdown before deciding whether a proposal is affordable.

Request clarity on:

- Agency management fees.
- Advertising spend.
- Content production.
- Software or platform subscriptions.
- Website or landing page work.
- Any setup or additional project fees.

For reference, Artlify's monthly retainers range from about $500 to $10,000, depending on the services, platforms and volume of work. Ad spend is paid separately, directly to the ad platforms.

Avoid comparing proposals by their final price alone. Check whether they include the same responsibilities, deliverables and level of support.

Ask what you would still need to pay for or organise yourself. A proposal becomes easier to assess when you can see the full commitment, including your own team's time.

## What questions should you ask on an agency discovery call?

Ask questions that reveal how the agency thinks, delivers work and handles uncertainty. Use the call to test whether its recommendations make sense for your business.

Start with these:

1. What do you need to understand before recommending a strategy?
2. Which problem would you investigate first?
3. What would you need from our team?
4. Who would manage the account and carry out the work?
5. How would you measure progress?
6. What would you do if the initial approach underperformed?
7. What is outside the proposed scope?
8. How would you communicate decisions and request approvals?

Give the agency room to ask questions too.

Listen for whether it explores your offer, margins, previous activity and sales process before prescribing services. Ask follow-up questions whenever an answer sounds impressive but leaves you unclear about what will happen.

## What should a marketing proposal include?

A useful proposal should explain the objective, deliverables, responsibilities, costs and measurement plan. You should be able to read it and understand what you are agreeing to buy.

Look for:

- **Objective:** The business problem the work addresses.
- **Scope:** Services and deliverables included.
- **Responsibilities:** What the agency handles and what you provide.
- **Schedule:** Milestones, dependencies and approval points.
- **Costs:** Fees, payment terms and separately funded expenses.
- **Reporting:** What will be reviewed and discussed.
- **Terms:** Contract length, cancellation and ownership.

Check ambiguous phrases such as "content support" or "full management". Ask the agency to define them.

If video is included, for example, does that mean planning, filming, editing or all three? Get the answer written into the scope before signing.

## How should an agency measure success?

Agree on measures that connect to your business goal, alongside the information needed to explain performance. Ask what each reported number will help you decide.

For a coaching business, you might agree to review qualified applications, booked calls, attendance and sales.

For an e-commerce brand, you might review orders, acquisition costs, order value and the costs involved in fulfilling those orders.

Define the terms together. What makes an enquiry qualified? Which expenses are included when assessing profitability? Who supplies the sales information?

Ask the agency to distinguish platform-reported results from your business records and explain any gaps. Reports should support a conversation about what to continue, change or investigate.

Agree on a realistic timeframe too. At Artlify, we review paid ads from the first 30 days, while social media usually takes around 90 days to show meaningful growth.

## What should happen if a campaign is not working?

Ask for a review process that investigates the problem and explains the next decision. Agree on how changes will be prioritised and communicated.

A practical process to discuss is:

1. Check that tracking and reporting are functioning.
2. Review the offer and message.
3. Examine the content or advertising.
4. Inspect the landing page or purchase process.
5. Review enquiry handling and follow-up.
6. Choose the next change and define how to assess it.

Ask what evidence would support each decision. If the agency proposes increasing spend, request an explanation of why that is appropriate.

You should understand what it has learned, what remains uncertain and what the next action is intended to resolve.

## What contract terms should you check before signing?

Check the scope, payment terms, cancellation process, ownership and handover arrangements before signing. Request written clarification wherever the agreement leaves an operational question unanswered.

Confirm:

- Who owns the accounts and completed assets.
- Who holds administrator access.
- Whether editable files are included.
- How revisions and extra work are handled.
- What happens when approvals are delayed.
- What notice is required to end the engagement.
- What the handover includes.

As an example, Artlify's minimum contract is three months, and after that you can cancel with one month's notice. When we finish working together, you receive a complete folder of all the marketing materials we created, plus your marketing strategy document.

Make sure the written agreement reflects what was discussed during the sales process.

## How do you make the final decision?

Choose the agency whose proposal, evidence and working process best fit your priorities. Use the same criteria to assess each candidate.

Write down your answers to these questions:

- Do they understand our main problem?
- Can they explain their recommendations clearly?
- Have they shown relevant work?
- Is the full financial commitment clear?
- Do we understand our responsibilities?
- Are we comfortable with the communication process?
- Are the terms acceptable?
